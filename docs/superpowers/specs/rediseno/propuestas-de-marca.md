# Veritas Lab Suite: personality proposals

## 1. Diagnosis: why it feels generic

- **Eyebrow** "Software para laboratorios · Datos · Calidad · Trazabilidad" is a list of keywords that any LIMS vendor could use. It says nothing about the person or the place.
- **H1** "Construyo software de laboratorio con IA, sabiendo dónde no ponerla": the main subject is AI, which is what every portfolio talks about in 2026. It defines him by a negative. He comes across as an AI developer who happens to work on labs, when he should come across as someone who builds lab software and happens to use AI. The line also feels odd because "ponerla" leaves the reader asking what "la" refers to.
- **Subtitle** ("Los cálculos que deciden son deterministas… audit trail") reads like a compliance policy. It's accurate, but it explains how the system behaves and gives no reason to hire Andrés.
- **The person is missing above the fold.** La Rioja only shows up in "Disponibilidad". There's no face, no place, no number, no story. The site's most distinctive facts (8 years of expedientes, a geography degree, the NOA region, 62/62 tests) are in the third or fourth section.
- **Trajectory**: "me enseñaron lo que exige un laboratorio regulado" stretches the claim, because a school is not a regulated lab. "Hoy lo aplico al software" is a flat close.
- **Buzzword pillars**: "Software con IA responsable". **Agency phrasing**: "Qué más puedo construir para tu laboratorio" and "¿…un proceso manual que duele?", which is a standard copywriting formula.
- **About**: "IA solo donde corresponde" and "Esa combinación de método, datos y normativa…" are abstract and could describe anyone.
- **Underused asset**: the module names (Enlace, Custodia, Curva, Margen) already have personality. The home copy never uses them that way.

## 2. Three directions

### A. "Verificado contra" (the one who verifies)
**Core idea:** The portfolio works like a calibration certificate. No claim appears without its reference, and every number names its test. His personality is that he is checkable.

**Tone rules**
- DO pair every claim with a source (NIST, EURACHEM, Westgard 1981, test file).
- DO use numbers instead of adjectives ("62/62", "100 000 valores", "k = 2").
- DO invite checking: "abrí el test", "compará".
- DON'T use qualitative self-praise ("robusto", "riguroso", "de calidad").
- DON'T state a claim that has no link behind it.

**Hero**
| | ES | EN |
|---|---|---|
| Eyebrow | Software para laboratorios · cada cálculo con su referencia | Laboratory software · every calculation with its reference |
| H1 | No me creas: cada cálculo trae su test. | Don't trust me. Check the test behind every number. |
| Subtitle | Westgard contra la publicación de 1981, la calibración contra NIST, la incertidumbre contra EURACHEM. 62 de 62 pruebas pasan, y podés abrir cada una. | Westgard against the 1981 paper, calibration against NIST, uncertainty against EURACHEM. 62 of 62 tests pass, and you can open every one. |
| CTAs | Abrir el dossier de validación · Probar los módulos | Open the validation dossier · Try the modules |

**Trajectory**
- ES: "Estudié estadística, climatología e hidrología como Profesor en Geografía: datos que tienen que cerrar. Después, ocho años de expedientes en escuelas de La Rioja me enseñaron que un registro sin respaldo no sirve. Este portfolio junta las dos cosas: cada afirmación lleva su referencia y su prueba."
- EN: "As a geography teacher I studied statistics, climatology and hydrology: data that has to add up. Then eight years of school records in La Rioja taught me that a record without backing is worthless. This portfolio brings both together: every claim carries its reference and its test."

**Section headings:** "Qué se verificó, y contra qué" · "Lo que decide el código, lo que redacta la IA" · "Evidencia, no promesas"
**Signature:** ES "Si no está verificado, no está terminado." / EN "If it isn't verified, it isn't done."

### B. "Del territorio a la muestra" (from the land to the sample)
**Core idea:** Before a sample reaches the lab it was water from a well, grape must, or drill core. A geographer from the NOA knows where samples come from and builds the software that follows them all the way to the signed report. This continues his mining portfolio ("Del territorio al código").

**Tone rules**
- DO name real places and matrices (pozo, mosto, testigo, salar, La Rioja, NOA).
- DO connect each module to a concrete physical sample.
- DO keep the regional framing sober and factual, not folkloric.
- DON'T imply lab bench experience he doesn't have. He knows the territory and the data, not the pipette.
- DON'T sell modules that are still "en desarrollo" (water, wine, geochemistry) as finished. Label them as roadmap.

I trained in geography, hydrology and geology; now I build the software that follows each sample to a signed report. |
| CTAs | Seguir una muestra · Ver lo que viene para el NOA | Follow a sample · See the regional roadmap |

**Trajectory**
- ES: "Soy Profesor en Geografía: estudié cartografía, geología, climatología e hidrología, el lugar de donde salen las muestras. Trabajé ocho años en la gestión administrativa de escuelas en La Rioja, cuidando expedientes y plazos. Hoy desarrollo software para el tramo que va de la muestra al dato firmado."

**Section headings:** "El recorrido de una muestra" · "Hecho en La Rioja, para cualquier laboratorio" · "Lo que viene: agua, vino y minería"
**Signature:** ES "De la muestra al informe, sin perder el rastro." / EN "From sample to report, without losing the trail."

*Risk:* today's demo is clinical (glucose, HL7). A clinical or pharma reader may feel this direction isn't aimed at them.

### C. "El expediente" (the paper-trail person)
**Core idea:** He spent eight years making sure every expediente said who, when and with what authorization. An audit trail is that same job done in software. This turns the "non-tech" background into the differentiator.

**Tone rules**
- DO speak in the first person, with short sentences and concrete everyday objects (expediente, firma, sello, plazo).
- DO translate regulatory concepts into what they protect ("quién, cuándo, qué cambió").
- DO keep a dry, slightly wry humor. No jokes about patients or results.
- DON'T overclaim ("una escuela es como un laboratorio regulado"). Say the habit carries over, not that the domain is the same.
- DON'T open with AI. It appears where it actually works: drafts with review.

|---|---|---|
| Eyebrow | Andrés Nahuel Páez · La Rioja, Argentina · Software para laboratorios | Andrés Nahuel Páez · La Rioja, Argentina · Laboratory software |
| H1 | Ocho años cuidando expedientes. Ahora construyo audit trails. | Eight years keeping school records. Now I build audit trails. |
| Subtitle | Sé qué pasa cuando un registro no dice quién, cuándo ni qué cambió. Acá cada cambio lo dice, y ningún resultado sale sin firma. | I know what happens when a record can't say who, when or what changed. Here every change does, and no result leaves unsigned. |
| CTAs | Seguir una muestra de punta a punta · Abrir el expediente de validación | Follow one sample end to end · Open the validation file |

**Trajectory**
- ES: "Entre 2017 y 2025 trabajé en la gestión administrativa de escuelas en La Rioja: expedientes, plazos, normativa. Ahí aprendí que un registro vale lo que vale su rastro. Antes me había formado como Profesor en Geografía, con estadística, geología e hidrología. Hoy junto las dos cosas en software: cálculos verificados contra referencias publicadas y registros que aguantan una auditoría."
- EN: "From 2017 to 2025 I worked in school administration in La Rioja: records, deadlines, regulations. That's where I learned a record is only as good as its trail. Before that I trained as a geography teacher, with statistics, geology and hydrology. Today I bring both into software: calculations verified against published references, and records that hold up to an audit."

**Section headings:** "Una muestra, del analizador al PDF firmado" · "Lo que queda registrado" · "Antes del código: expedientes y mapas"
**Signature:** ES "Registros que aguantan una auditoría." / EN "Records that hold up to an audit."

## 3. Recommendation

**Use C for the hero and trajectory, A as the voice of the rest of the site, and B as one regional section.**

- **Why C leads:** it's the only direction no other developer can copy. It's true and specific, and it makes the career change a strength instead of something to explain away. "Expediente" lands immediately with any Argentine reader. "Records → audit trails" is clear to any GxP/QA reader worldwide, because records management *is* their world.
- **National (Argentina):** C is warm and recognizable, and A's references (ANMAT 4159/2023, ISO 17025) give it credibility with INTI, CONICET and private labs.
- **Local NOA/Cuyo:** a B section below the flow ("Hecho en La Rioja, para cualquier laboratorio" plus the water/arsenic, INV wine and mining roadmap, clearly marked "en desarrollo"). Use B's hero as the opening line in direct outreach to La Rioja, Catamarca, San Juan and Mendoza labs.
- **Worldwide/remote:** C's H1 works in English without the Argentine context, and A's "Don't trust me. Check the test" fits how QA and CSV reviewers think. Avoid B as the global hero, because the local matrices mean little to a European pharma reader.
- AI moves out of the H1 and into a small, confident line in the flow: "La IA redacta borradores; los resultados los calcula código con tests."
